# Implementation backlog and pilot validation

This is a dependency-ordered plan for a small team, not a delivery-date estimate. P0 means required before real student transport; P1 means valuable after the controlled pilot prerequisites are met. The first buildable slice is B01–B06, entirely synthetic. B07–B12 complete the pilot operating capability. B13 is a gated experiment; B14 is later expansion.

Suggested team responsibilities: one engineer owns command/database integrity, one owns mobile/web flows, and a product/operations lead owns policies and school/operator coordination. These are responsibilities, not a claim that three people can staff transport and incident coverage alone. Use specialist legal, insurance and security review at the corresponding gates. No agent delegation or new project tasks are created by this document.

## First buildable vertical slice

Create synthetic School A and School B, one School A guardian, child, accepted driver/vehicle, teacher, gate account and receiving adult. The guardian approves the exact trip revision; driver accepts; teacher marks ready; gate checks arrival and allocates a bay; paired school handoff puts the child onboard; driver starts the trip and parks at the synthetic destination; the authorized recipient and driver record receipt. Parent, driver and school see consistent authorized status, timestamps and audit history. A disconnected parent catches up by fetching current state.

The second demonstration revokes approval while two gate devices attempt release. The database permits at most one correctly authorized transition, or denies both after revocation; it never releases twice. The third demonstrates absent recipient and keeps the child with the driver until verified recovery. These are stronger acceptance demonstrations than a map animation or a collection of static role dashboards.

The initial UI can be plain but must show child and driver identity, current permission, next action, freshness, confirmation state and the safe action on failure. Use synthetic accounts with real authorization boundaries; a role dropdown alone is not a security test. No payment integration, route optimization, speed API or public deployment is required for this slice.

## Test specifications

Use a real local PostgreSQL transaction engine for integrity tests, not only mocked repositories. Use two independent authenticated connections and explicit concurrency barriers to force race order. Verify persisted state, receipts, evidence, audit/outbox cardinality and authorized projections. Property-based state sequences should never produce two current confirmed holders, two open transfers, or delivery without evidence. Manual reconciliation must preserve uncertainty instead of inventing a holder.

| Test | Setup and action | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T01 Duplicate school release | Send the same finalize command twice with one key; repeat using two different keys and simultaneous gate devices | Same key yields one logical result; different keys yield one commit and conflict/rejection. Exactly one outbound custody event and one passenger transition; no second notification domain event |
| T02 Concurrent actions | Two visits claim one bay; teacher withdraws readiness while gate prepares release; two manifest edits compete | At most one bay holder; readiness/prepare serialized; stale revision rejected; all writers use common locks. No lost update or partial sibling manifest |
| T03 Revoked authorization | Barrier-controlled revoke before finalize, finalize before revoke, and revoke during pending physical handoff; replay old challenge/JWT | Revoke-first denies; finalize-first reports existing driver custody and creates intervention; pending physical transfer becomes disputed and non-releasable; stale credentials cannot restore approval |
| T04 Cross-school/access | School A staff/guardian/driver requests School B resources by guessed IDs, changes nested foreign IDs, subscribes to channel, requests download/export | No data leakage or mutation through API, database client roles, private storage, realtime or export; global driver identity does not grant school membership; no sensitive error existence leak |
| T05 Absent recipient | Driver marks arrival, supplies own/replayed/wrong-recipient code, then declares delivered; authorized recipient absent | Remains at-stop/recovery with driver responsible; no delivered event. Backup needs valid scope/verification; return requires confirmed staffed receiver; completion only after recorded alternate handoff |
| T06 Reconnect and lost acknowledgement | Commit a release but drop HTTP response; suppress, duplicate and reorder websocket hints; log out or revoke while client offline | Receipt lookup discovers committed release; refreshed versions converge without rewind; no second release; inaccessible cached records cleared on session change; notification arrival alone never marks custody |
| T07 Tracking loss | Deny background permission, kill app, lock phone, cut network, change device lease, then upload old and duplicate samples | Health becomes stale/unavailable; no fake live motion; escalation to staffed owner; old samples historical; replay deduped; no trip auto-completion, speeding penalty or loss of custody accountability |
| T08 Physical/database gap | Prepare transfer, simulate boarding, then cause expiry/revocation/timeout before final recording | Pending/disputed state and no additional release; staff must reconcile actual holder; timeout cannot reset to school custody; evidence retains physical and recorded times |
| T09 Siblings/no-shows | Two siblings, only one ready/approved; driver cancels, fails to arrive, or one child becomes absent | No group release shortcut; every child gets individual disposition; boarded child cannot be cancelled out of history; guardian and staff must resolve remaining child before departure |
| T10 Eligibility and restrictions | Expire a vehicle credential at release boundary; suspend driver while onboard; attempt reinstatement as ordinary support | New release denied if expiry/suspension wins; already-boarded children trigger supervised recovery; minimum accountable trip scope persists under safety policy; reinstatement requires safety role/reason |

Credible sustained case creates only a review candidate with evidence and algorithm version; no automatic finding/ban |

Also test CSRF on browser mutations, missing/expired identity, privilege escalation, revoked guardian authority with otherwise current trip approval, brute-force challenges, notification retry, command payload mismatch, support access without case purpose, and last-child delivery ending tracking. Bound the testing to these safety and consistency risks; avoid snapshot tests that merely duplicate component markup.

Native field matrix must identify device model, OS, app build, permission settings, test route, network/battery conditions and observed gaps. Test both iOS and Android, including background navigation to another app. Record what was not tested. No physical iOS build, signing, App Store acceptance or background reliability is implied by writing this plan or running web tests.

## Pilot measurement plan

Collect a baseline under the school's existing process before PICK. Proposed observation window: a complete ordinary school week, extending if weather, attendance or special events make it unrepresentative. Choose comparable pilot days/time windows and report context, staff numbers, bay configuration and participating share. This is a recommended method, not evidence already collected. Avoid attributing all differences to PICK if staffing, roadworks or dismissal policy changed.

Use trained observation at a fixed physical queue boundary for both baseline and pilot; app check-in alone can miss vehicles already waiting in the street. Record school-local service date with UTC timestamps, vehicle visit IDs without public plates, child/visit counts, bay entry/exit and exceptions. Obtain school-approved data collection procedures; use minimal information. Compare all dismissal traffic, not only the easiest enrolled rides.

| Metric | Definition and denominator | Interpretation/limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Median and p95 queue wait | `bay_entry − physical_queue_entry` per vehicle visit; report sample size and predeclared quantile method (nearest rank for p95) | Show exception and abandoned/censored visits separately; don't remove slow cases to improve result; also report total time to site exit |
| Throughput | Vehicles exited and children handed off per supervised dismissal minute, by bay and school total | More children per car differs from more cars per minute; record active staffing/bays |
| Street spillback | Observed maximum queue length and minutes beyond school-approved boundary, plus blocked crossings/intersections | Manual observer/site sensor method consistent across periods; moving the queue off-screen is not improvement |
| Handoff exceptions | Exception transfers/attempts divided by all handoff attempts; school and home separately, by cause/severity | Include no recipient, authority mismatch and uncertain custody; higher reporting completeness may initially increase counts |
| On-time trips | Departures and verified arrivals within agreed windows / scheduled eligible trips; show cancellations/no-shows separately | Predeclare windows and whether denominator includes cancelled bookings; display both scheduled and completed counts to prevent survivorship bias |
| Location telemetry coverage | Active onboard minutes with a valid sample no older than the agreed freshness bound / total onboard minutes | Define freshness bound from device tests before pilot; gaps count as unavailable, not safe |
| Speed-limit coverage | Moving observed time with reliable applicable limit and road match / moving observed time; also show share of total onboard time unobserved | Separate from location coverage and candidate rate; unknown segments cannot support safety claims |
| Safety incidents | Counts and severity, allegations vs substantiated findings; exposure as passenger trips and passenger-hours | Serious cases reviewed individually; small pilot or zero observed incidents cannot establish a safety rate advantage |
| Vehicle volume and occupancy | Total pickup vehicle visits / dismissed children; transported children per PICK visit; repeat visits counted | Necessary to evaluate actual traffic reduction; sibling pooling may help but is not evidence of school-wide reduction |
| Operational burden | Staff minutes per session/exception, support cases per trip, child supervised wait and total ride time | Congestion improvement bought with excessive child wait or staff burden is not an unqualified success |
| Reliability | Command failure/conflict rate, unresolved transfer age, notification delay and connected-view convergence | Measures software/operations, not proof that physical handoffs were safe |

Raw baseline and pilot observations should produce a reproducible report with period, inclusion/exclusion rules, missing-data counts, denominators and uncertainty. For p95 with a small sample, show the sample size and avoid strong claims. Prefer matched-day comparisons and stratify by occupancy/participation rather than promising causal proof from a small uncontrolled pilot.

**Go/no-go:** school, operator and safety owner agree acceptable numerical queue, punctuality, freshness and workload thresholds after baseline and device tests, before live operation. Record them as versioned pilot criteria; do not tune them after results to claim success. Safety halt rules in document 04 apply independently. Expand only if custody exceptions are resolved, operational capacity is adequate and measured traffic benefits justify expansion. Otherwise adjust the process, keep the service constrained or stop it.

No tests in this file have been executed: they are acceptance specifications for the future implementation. Document integrity checks are separate from application, device and operational validation.
